## Abstract

Age-related alteration of intracellular processes has been recognized as tipping
of homeostatic balance towards increased entropy, reflected in hallmarks of aging
such as the loss of epigenetic information and disruption of proteostasis.
Although the hallmarks of aging are useful for contextualizing geroscience
research, the framework itself does not explain what fundamentally causes them to
emerge in the first place. The theorem aims to explain the emergence of the
hallmarks of aging as phenomena **secondary to extracellular matrix chemical
crosslinking**, suggesting that the ensuant age-related ECM stiffening is a
causative upstream agent in the aging process.

Keywords: aging · entropy · extracellular matrix

## The argument

1. **The hallmarks framework is descriptive, not causal.** Mechanisms of aging
   have been organized into the hallmarks framework (López-Otín et al., 2023).
   It is an excellent introduction to biogerontology but does not amount to a
   full scientific paradigm; it omits prominent characteristics of aging and
   does not explain why the hallmarks emerge (Gems & de Magalhães, 2021;
   Keshavarz et al., 2022).

2. **Biomechanics is the neglected variable.** Mechanical properties of cells and
   their environment are recognized as important across a wide range of
   age-related derailments (Phillip et al., 2015), yet the field omits ECM
   biomechanics from the hallmarks list. The author attributes this to a
   predominantly **cell-centric** view of aging, and argues that a view of tissue
   aging that ignores the cellular environment is incomplete.

3. **Crosslink accumulation is clock-like.** Some chemical damage to ECM
   components — specifically **sugar-derived crosslinks** — accrues linearly with
   age and, once normalized by component tissue residence time (turnover),
   proceeds at the same rate regardless of location in the body (Verzijl et al.,
   2000; Monnier & Sell, 2006). This location-independence is central: it makes
   crosslinking a candidate universal driver rather than a tissue-specific effect.

4. **Aging as entropy.** Aging has been conceptualized both as time-dependent
   damage accumulation (Moqri et al., 2023) and as an increase in intracellular
   entropy (Lu et al., 2023), with no field consensus on a definition
   (Gladyshev et al., 2024). DEATh reinterprets the hallmarks as **emergent and
   subordinate entropic events** relative to ECM stiffening driven by crosslink
   accumulation. Hence *delocalized* entropy: the entropic source sits outside
   the cell, in the matrix.

## Why it matters for the rest of the work

If ECM crosslinking is upstream, then interventions that clear or reverse
crosslinks are upstream interventions. This is the through-line connecting the
projects: AGE-degrading enzymes and engineered MMPs attack the crosslinks
directly; the ECM Aging Atlas supplies the matrisome baseline needed to measure
change; ribosome glycation extends the same glycation chemistry into proteostasis.

## Representing this accurately

DEATh is a **proposed theorem** authored by Rakhan Aimbetov. It is a stated
research position, not established consensus in biogerontology. The supporting
citations above are real literature marshalled in support of the argument; they
do not themselves endorse the theorem. Present it as a hypothesis with a defined
mechanism and testable consequences.
